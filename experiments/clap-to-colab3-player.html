Show Summary

In this installment of Clap to COLAB, Kaspar König takes us deep into the Swiss Alps for the 21st annual Homemade Festival—a temporary hacker's utopia hosted by the Swiss Mechatronic Art Society (SGMK). From anxious debates on AI surveillance in corporate culture to anti-capitalist 'Klebtops' and cross-stitched automotive pixel art, this episode captures the raw, unfiltered 'Do It With Others' (DIWO) spirit of a community dedicated to turning discarded technology into chaotic, beautiful art.

Host: Kaspar König
Station: Collaboradio.org (Freie Radios Berlin Brandenburg)

🎙️ Key Interviews & Segments

1. The "SCHAISS" Workshop (Shit AI)

The episode starts with a group discussing the implications of AI voice recognition in the corporate world. They point out how AI can now transcribe and log every single word said in meetings, turning casual conversations into searchable "Big Data" that lawyers can use, fundamentally changing how freely people can speak.

2. Pije: Anti-Capitalist Hardware ("The Wrap Top")

Pije introduces their 15-year-old "wrap top" (laptop) which they modify as a philosophical rejection of modern capitalist design choices (specifically Apple). By gluing/taping materials onto it, Pije attempts to make the old machine beautiful on their own terms, jokingly calling it a "Klebtop" (from German 'kleben' for gluing) instead of a laptop.

3. Robotics and Data Art (Gandalf & Valentina)

Gandalf: A long-time attendee who runs "Sticker Central" and creates "Bruschographie"—an aquarelle painting robot that automates the physical painting process from digital designs.
Valentina: Explains her work in "data art," where she collaborates with scientists to turn raw data into 3D-printed sculptures and code-based visuals. She highlights the festival as a democratic, self-organized utopia where people can escape their "normal jobs" to be truly free.

4. Bricolage Universal (Flo Kaufmann)

Flo, a sound and electronics artist, discusses his "Bricolage" approach: repurposing and abusing found objects with a childish curiosity. At the festival, he shared a simplified method for developing black-and-white film without a darkroom using highly diluted, long-lasting chemistry, as well as an arpeggiator kit.

5. The "Simulation of Utopia" (Alwin Weber)

Alwin, a circuit-bending enthusiast from Dresden, describes the Homemade festival as an inspiring "simulation of utopia." He notes how people with different "superpowers" (biohacking, coding, soldering, social skills) gather to build things and solve problems together. His first visit in 2010 inspired him to start a similar festival, Circuit Control, in Dresden.

6. Olsen: Automotive Pixel Art and "Ghost Sounds"

Olsen describes an ongoing deterministic art project: using a custom script to generate pixel-art of tuned car tailpipes, which is then recreated by hand using cross-stitching yarn to make cushions. The project explores the car as an object of religious worship and technological fetishization in modern society.

7. The Origins of Homemade (Oli Jäggi)

Oli, an organizer and electronic music enthusiast, explains his journey into the community through building audio performance circuits. He notes that the festival was originally started by Migros Kulturprozent before being taken over by the SGMK, marking its 21st edition this year with an influx of new, younger participants.

8. Micro-Sound & Resonance (Taras)

Taras, originally from Ukraine, discusses his journey from acoustic guitar to drone music, abstract photography, and "micro-sound." He describes his highly precise, custom-built instruments (using rubber bands, vibration generators, and ultra-close micing) to explore the multi-dimensional nature of tiny resonances. He described his first time at the festival as a "life-changing event."

🤖 About the Production Process

This project is an exploration of human-AI collaboration. The original radio broadcasts (approx. 3 hours of audio) were automatically transcribed using a locally-run WhisperX model to preserve data privacy.

Those raw transcripts were then processed by Google's Gemini AI to extract the chapter timelines, resolve speaker identities, and generate the episode summaries. Finally, the entire web experience—from the custom audio players to the 'glassmorphism' UI design—was collaboratively coded using the Antigravity AI coding assistant pair-programming with the creator.

Approximate Compute & Ecological Footprint:

  • Token Usage: ~1,000,000 input tokens (due to the AI repeatedly reading 45,000+ words of transcripts in context) and ~30,000 output tokens (writing the code, HTML, and summaries).
  • Estimated Compute Cost: ~$4.00 USD (equivalent API cost for the LLM).
  • Energy Consumption: ~0.3 kWh (Local GPU transcription + Cloud AI inference) — Roughly equivalent to boiling a kettle of water 3 times.

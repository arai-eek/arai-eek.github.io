Show Summary: "Clap to COLAB - Part 3"

In this installment of Clap to COLAB, Kaspar König takes us deep into the Swiss Alps for the 21st annual Homemade Festival—a temporary hacker's utopia hosted by the Swiss Mechatronic Art Society (SGMK). From anxious debates on AI surveillance in corporate culture to anti-capitalist 'Klebtops' and cross-stitched automotive pixel art, this episode captures the raw, unfiltered 'Do It With Others' (DIWO) spirit of a community dedicated to turning discarded technology into chaotic, beautiful art.

Host: Kaspar König
Station: Collaboradio.org (Freie Radios Berlin Brandenburg)

🎙️ Key Interviews & Segments

1. The "SCHAISS" Workshop (Shit AI)

The episode starts with a group discussing the implications of AI voice recognition in the corporate world. They point out how AI can now transcribe and log every single word said in meetings, turning casual conversations into searchable "Big Data" that lawyers can use, fundamentally changing how freely people can speak.

2. Pije: Anti-Capitalist Hardware ("The Wrap Top")

Pije introduces their 15-year-old "wrap top" (laptop) which they modify as a philosophical rejection of modern capitalist design choices. By gluing/taping materials onto it, Pije attempts to make the old machine beautiful on their own terms, jokingly calling it a "Klebtop".

3. Robotics and Data Art (Gandalf & Valentina)

Gandalf runs "Sticker Central" and creates "Bruschographie"—an aquarelle painting robot. Valentina explains her work in "data art," collaborating with scientists to turn raw data into 3D-printed sculptures and code-based visuals. She highlights the festival as a democratic, self-organized utopia.

4. Bricolage Universal (Flo Kaufmann)

Flo, a sound and electronics artist, discusses his "Bricolage" approach: repurposing and abusing found objects with a childish curiosity. At the festival, he shared a simplified method for developing black-and-white film without a darkroom using highly diluted chemistry.

5. The "Simulation of Utopia" (Alwin Weber)

Alwin, a circuit-bending enthusiast from Dresden, describes the Homemade festival as an inspiring "simulation of utopia" where people with different superpowers (biohacking, coding, soldering) gather to solve problems together.

6. Olsen: Automotive Pixel Art and "Ghost Sounds"

Olsen describes an ongoing deterministic art project: using a custom script to generate pixel-art of tuned car tailpipes, which is then recreated by hand using cross-stitching yarn to make cushions.

7. The Origins of Homemade (Oli Jäggi)

Oli, an organizer and electronic music enthusiast, explains his journey into the community through building audio performance circuits and discusses the 21-year history of the Homemade festival.

8. Micro-Sound & Resonance (Taras)

Taras discusses his journey from acoustic guitar to drone music, abstract photography, and "micro-sound." He describes his highly precise, custom-built instruments (using rubber bands and touch micing) to explore the multi-dimensional nature of tiny resonances.

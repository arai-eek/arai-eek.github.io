1 Manual — drive the grid with one word

Tab: 1. visual (manual) · ~10 min · zero formulas to write

The first tab is alive already. There's a 30×30 grid of colored squares, and one yellow cell on the right with the word สวัสดี in it. That's the only thing you control.

The mechanic

Every cell in the grid runs the exact same formula:

=RANDBETWEEN(0, LEN($AF$2))

Translation: "give me a random integer between 0 and the length of cell AF2's text". Twelve conditional-formatting rules map each integer (0..11) to a color. LEN("สวัสดี") = 6, so cells take values 0–6, lighting up the first 7 colors.

Spreadsheets recalculate every time anything changes. So every edit = a new random draw = a new visual. That's your frame rate.

2 Formula — same trick, fancier math

Tab: 2. mandala (formula) · ~20 min · turn random into structure

Same idea — one formula, every cell, 12 colors. But the formula now uses each cell's position to compute the color, not random. Result: a structured pattern instead of noise.

The mechanic

=MOD(
  ABS(ROUND(
    SQRT((ROW()-25.5)^2 + (COLUMN()-40.5)^2) * $CE$2   ← distance from center × ring spacing
    - $CE$9                                              ← - time (animation)
    + $CE$3 * COS(ATAN2(COLUMN()-40.5, ROW()-25.5)
                  * $CE$4 + $CE$5)                       ← petal warp × twist
  )),
  $CE$6                                                  ← mod base = palette size
)

3 AI Lab — ask your AI for a formula you couldn't write

Tab: 3. AI lab · ~30 min · bring your own AI

The grid is empty. The knob panel is set up: 5 knobs, a seed-word cell, a time cell. Conditional formatting is already in place — paste any formula that returns 0–11 and the colors light up automatically.

Your job: tell your AI what pattern you want. Their job: write the formula. You paste it into A1, hit Cmd/Ctrl+A then paste-fill the whole grid.

⚠️ #1 thing that goes wrong — read this first:
If you paste an AI formula and no colors appear (you see numbers or a blank grid), the formula is returning decimals or numbers outside 0–11. The 12 conditional-formatting rules match integers exactly.

Fix: wrap your AI's formula in this clamp: 
=MOD(INT( YOUR_AI_FORMULA ), 12)
That forces the output to an integer in 0..11. Works every time. Make this your default paste pattern.

What to do when your AI lies

It will. Spreadsheet formulas are precise and AIs sometimes hallucinate function names or argument orders. The workshop loop:

  1. Paste formula into A1. If it works → fill the grid.
  2. If it shows #NAME? or #ERROR! → click the cell, click the little orange error indicator, copy the error text.
  3. Paste the error back into your AI: "this gave me [error]. Fix it."
  4. Try again. Usually works on attempt 2 or 3.
If your AI keeps failing: ask it explicitly for "Google Sheets formula syntax, not Excel." Most AIs default to Excel which uses some different function names.

🛠 Stuck?

The grid shows numbers (or nothing), not colors

This is the #1 thing that goes wrong — covered up in Block 3, repeated here. Wrap your formula:

=MOD(INT( YOUR_FORMULA ), 12)

Why: 12 CF rules use NUMBER_EQ "0"..."11" — exact integer match. Decimals (5.0001) or out-of-range (15) won't match. INT(...) forces integer; MOD(..., 12) clamps to 0..11. Other things to check:

  • Conditional formatting is missing — Format → Conditional formatting; expect 12 rules
  • You pasted into the wrong tab (no CF set up there)
  • The formula returns text — wrap in VALUE(...) to force numeric

Numbers are visible inside the cells

The "hide numbers" trick is missing. In every CF rule, set text color = background color. Numbers stay there for the formula but become invisible.

The whole grid is one color

Your formula returns a constant. Check if it actually uses ROW() and COLUMN(), or your random source (LEN, RANDBETWEEN, time cell).

My sheet feels slow

You probably pasted the formula into way more cells than the visible grid. Trim the grid to ~40×50 — that's 2,000 cells, still fast.

I broke something — how do I get back?

Cmd/Ctrl+Z as many times as you need. Or revisit the starter copy URL and make a fresh one.

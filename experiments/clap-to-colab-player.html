Show Summary

In this episode of Clap to COLAB, host Kaspar König dives into the world of "Technobiological Futures," a project initiated by Marc Dusseiller (dusjagr) and the Hackteria network. From the Swiss Alps to Chiang Mai, the guests discuss the intersection of art, technology, and biology. Expect deep philosophical dives into anti-eugenics bioart with "ugly punk babies," the importance of durational workshops over "fast-food" art, and animating children's paintings in Thailand—all wrapped up in Kaspar’s pandemic-survival beats and experimental acoustic recordings from an empty gas sphere.

Host: Kaspar König
Station: Collaboradio.org (Freie Radios Berlin Brandenburg)

🎙️ Key Interviews & Segments

1. Marc Dusseiller (dusjagr): "Make Friends, Not Art"

Marc Dusseiller (also known as dusjagr or Pakde Marcjono) reflects on his 20-year journey as a "workshopologist" bringing people together from different cultural and scientific backgrounds. Rather than "fast-food" one-day events, he advocates for long-duration "CoLabs" (co-laboratories) that foster lasting human connections. His core philosophy for these gatherings is: "Make friends, not art." He emphasizes the increasing merger of technology with the human body and the living environment.

2. Adam Zaretsky: Bioart and the Aesthetics of Otherness

Adam runs a "wet and juicy" bioart lab where he conducts hands-on workshops on genetic modification (often using embryos, germ cells, and "DNA cupcakes"). He uses his work to question the trajectory of biotechnology, specifically targeting the "neo-fascist breeding" and eugenics pursued by transhumanist oligarchs aiming for perfection. Instead, Adam advocates for the aesthetics of non-perfection, otherness, and what he playfully calls "ugly punk babies."

3. Kaspar König: Pandemic Beats and Acoustic Discoveries

Between interviews, Kaspar shares his own sonic explorations. He details how he survived the isolation of the Corona pandemic by reversing his daily to-do list: he started every morning at 8:00 AM by making electronic beats on his machines to cheer himself up. He also shares unique acoustic recordings, including a vocal session with Julia Hodl inside a massive, empty gas storage sphere in Schaffhausen, and the sounds of a "grown tube" made from 3D-printed valves and reeds on a long stick.

4. Wave (Tomorrow Lab, Chiang Mai)

Wave, an engineering graduate who returned to Chiang Mai, Thailand, shares how he built a local collective called "Tomorrow Lab." He focuses on bringing technology and media art into the local community. Notable projects include animating discarded paintings from young schoolchildren (inspired by digital Van Gogh exhibitions) and "Midnight Ride," a media art integration into the traditional midnight Lantern Festival at the local temple.

🤖 About the Production Process

This project is an exploration of human-AI collaboration. The original radio broadcasts (approx. 3 hours of audio) were automatically transcribed using a locally-run WhisperX model to preserve data privacy.

Those raw transcripts were then processed by Google's Gemini AI to extract the chapter timelines, resolve speaker identities, and generate the episode summaries. Finally, the entire web experience—from the custom audio players to the 'glassmorphism' UI design—was collaboratively coded using the Antigravity AI coding assistant pair-programming with the creator.

Approximate Compute & Ecological Footprint:

  • Token Usage: ~1,000,000 input tokens (due to the AI repeatedly reading 45,000+ words of transcripts in context) and ~30,000 output tokens (writing the code, HTML, and summaries).
  • Estimated Compute Cost: ~$4.00 USD (equivalent API cost for the LLM).
  • Energy Consumption: ~0.3 kWh (Local GPU transcription + Cloud AI inference) — Roughly equivalent to boiling a kettle of water 3 times.

This project is partially supported through the “Technobiological Futures” programme (February 2026 — August 2027), a multi-phase initiative spanning Thailand, Switzerland, Indonesia, and India. Co-funded by “Synergies”, by the Swiss Arts Council Pro Helvetia, the Co-Laboratories catalyse South-South collaborations that bypass traditional North-mediated routes, building sustainable networks for art-science-technology exchange across the Global South. Connecting the Global Hackteria Network, SGMK (Swiss Mechatronic Art Society), Tomorrow.lab. Embracing a Do-It-With-Others (DIWO) approach to collective experimentation across multiple perspectives.
